## The Story

So after my first foray into this world of fermenting things (I made a Finnish Sima, which you can
read about [here](@/blog/2013-12-the-lemons-started-it-all-sima/index.md)), I decided to delve a
little deeper in this new domain. Since I still had a bunch of Meyer lemons that I needed/wanted to
use up, and was intrigued by the idea of mead, my interwebbing lead me to find
[this recipe for Lemon Mead](http://www.joyofwine.net/vinlemonmead.htm). The recipe is nice and
descriptive, encourages the use of fresh fruit (which I like the idea of much more than just buying
juice from the store), and was for a single gallon instead of the typical five-gallon recipes I
seemed to find. With that, I was off to prepare for this new experiment.

First things first, I needed a one gallon [carboy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboy) (aka a glass
jug) to let the magic happen in. I didn’t want to get too crazy with this whole thing, so doing a
single gallon seemed like a good place to start. Since I was impatient, I just ran to Whole Foods to
see if they carried any juices in one-gallon glass carboys. Sure enough, they did, and at $8, it
actually turns out to be cheaper than just buying an empty carboy of the same size! WF actually sold
empty carboys on the next isle over for $13;
[here’s one on Amazon for $13](http://www.amazon.com/Midwest-Homebrewing-and-Winemaking-Supplies/dp/B0064O8Z76)...

Next, I needed some honey. Since I was already at Whole Foods, I picked out the most local,
cheapest, most untreated honey I could find, which turned out to be Orange Blossom from
[Gipson’s Golden](http://www.gipsonsgolden.com/). They were sold in 24 oz. bottles, so I got 2;
that’s 3 lbs., although I planned on using less due to the sweetness of the Meyers.

All ready!

I’ll list the original ingredient list here, but I made some adjustments; follow the link above for
the full recipe.

## Original Ingredients:

- 3 lbs. light-colored honey
- about 3 quarts of water
- 6-8 homegrown or organic lemons
- 1 tsp. yeast nutrient
- 1 crushed Campden tablet (optional)
- 1 packet mead yeast if you can get it, or any other wine yeast
- Potassium metabisulfate (instead of Campden tabs)

## My ingredients:

- 8 [Meyer lemons](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyer_lemon)
- 1.5 bottles 24 oz. Gipson’s Golden orange honey Gipson's Golden orange honey
- 1/2 c. brown sugar
- 1 t. [baker’s sugar](http://www.chsugar.com/sugar/detail/bakers-sugar)
- ~3.5 L water
- ~8 oz. apple juice
- 1 packet [Red Star Premier Cuvée](http://www.midwestsupplies.com/red-star-premier-cuvee.html)

For some reason, I felt inclined to add in the brown sugar; maybe it was to compliment the apple
juice? I added in some of the apple juice that came in the carboy–mostly just so I didn’t feel
absolutely wasteful by dumping the juice down the drain.

## Log

### 1/18, 2:30pm.

Boil all. Scrape off crap with a ladle. Don’t boil too hard. Add the lemon peel (from all 8 lemons),
then the juice (from 4 or 5. Doing more since the Meyer lemons are so tasty). Let cool to `80F`.

<image src="Stirring.jpg" width="100%">

## 9pm.

SG: 1.072. Took ~75F, so add 0.0018 (http://www.howtobrew.com/appendices/appendixA.html), 1.0738.

Pitched Red Star Premier Cuvée: added to 8oz water at 100F for 15m. Added ~1tsp bakers sugar. Let
sit for ~30m (it foamed, yay!).

Add yeast mix to the must (was in stainless steel, migrated to enamel dutch oven) and mostly covered
with the dutch oven lid.

## 10:30pm.

Added to carboy. Used balloon as airlock. Stored in office closet.

## 1/19, 10:25am.

Added raisins. Bubbles and foam!
