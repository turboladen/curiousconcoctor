2 batches of the same variety of peaches. One washed in chlorine dioxide, the other with soap and
water. About 25lbs of the former (had to throw one tray out since the package wasn’t sealed), and 30
of the latter. I’m going to do the same recipe for each, starting at the same time and bottling at
the same time. Since the ones of these I’ve done in the past I’ve always just washed in the sink
with fruit & veggie wash and they’ve turned out super sour, I’d like to know if there’s residual
yeast on them that causes that. The peaches washed with ClO2 should be devoid of that residual
yeast. All of these peaches have been frozen since around July.

## Recipe

- 25-30 lbs peaches, sliced, with skin. Include skins from 1/2 of those.
- 3 Honeycrisp apples (no Pink Ladies available)
- 1 Valencia orange (will use juice and peel)
- 1 lemon, juice
- Lalvin D47
- 2t Pectic enzyme
- 5G painters bag

Did ClO2 batch first (since it’s the clean one). Juiced all peaches. Added pulp/skins from 3/5 trays
worth of peaches. Added apple juice, orange juice, and lemon juice (no pulp). Added skin from about
2/3 of the orange. This totaled what looked like just under 3 gallons. Added water to bring it up to
about 3.75G. Added pectic enzyme (dissolved in about 1/4c warm water). Stirred. After sitting a bit,
looks like the internal temp on this one is about 79F.

For the control… juiced all peaches. Added pulp/skins from 4/6 trays worth of peaches. Same for the
rest. Oddly, 6 trays yielded about the same as 5 of the ClO2 trays.

The peaches and their juice from the ClO2 batch seemed a bit more crisp and preserved than the
others. The control was oxidized and had a bit of funk on the smell and taste. Both were still tart;
tart enough to expect good taste from their results.
