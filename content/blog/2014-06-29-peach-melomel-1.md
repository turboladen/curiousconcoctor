## The Story

I recently concocted a Nectarine Cider, which had quite an interesting development. All through the
fermentation, it smelled and tasted great–really fragrant, great sugar/sour balance, good body–which
got me thinking that I needed to do a simple mead in a similar fashion: all fresh fruit and fruit
juice (i.e. I juice the fresh fruit and don’t add water), plus the yeast, plus the honey. I was
intending on doing this with nectarines, since I really like the extra fragrances and tang that come
with them, but my wife convinced me (it didn’t take much) to go with peaches instead this time. My
plans changed, though, when I went to rack the nectarine cider for the first time.

Before I get into that though, I should put out the disclaimer: I haven’t done my due diligence in
researching this, so the jury is still out as to whether one can _technically_ make a cider from
anything but apples, so I’m just gonna roll with calling it a cider for now.

So before I racked the nectarine cider out of the fermenter, I, naturally, pulled a taste. It was
kind of a bummer too, since my half-sister was visiting, and asked if she’d like a taste (I’m
thinking that it’s gonna taste as good as it had, of course). She obliged. The cider was foul. When
I pulled the top off of the Brew Bucket, I couldn’t help but wonder how the white, chalky, scaly
stuff got on the krausen and wonder what the heck it was. Since I wasn’t sure if I’d ruined the
cider or not, it seemed liked I should play it safe and scale back on the fruit for the mead. This
is a bit of a bummer too as I’ve had a bunch of the nectarine cider and thoroughly enjoyed
it–meaning that I could’ve probably done well to use more fruit in this mead. All that to say that
this mead deviated a bit from the original plan, and won’t be quite as comparable to the cider as I
would’ve liked (just because I’m curious), but it’ll still be tasty.

## Recipe

- ~15 lbs. July Flame/Summer Flame Peaches
- 2 tsp. Pectic Enzyme
- 2 handfuls raisins
- 1 G. Orange Blossom honey from [Shamrock S Bees](http://www.shamrocksbees.com/)
- [Lalvin D47](http://www.lalvinyeast.com/D47.asp)

### 6/29.

9lbs or the peaches had been pitted and frozen, so I thawed those in the sink; poured hot water on
them and left them there for about and hour. Got almost all defrosted, then decided to put all the
peaches in a pot and warm them, just to speed things up. Once about room temp, I was ready to start
juicing.

Poured all the honey into the fermentor. Rinsed the honey jug with about a gallon of filtered water
to get the remains of the honey. Added raisins.

Lined the fermenter with a 5G painters bag. Juiced all peaches and added them. Added ~2L of the
skins/pulp. Stirred for days with the baster. Took a gravity reading and it was off the scale!
Tasted, and it seemed like it was all honey and water, so I stirred some more.

TODO: [gravity]

Mixed the pectic enzyme with about 4oz. tap water, added, and mixed for about another 5 minutes.
`SG: 1.202`, at about 80F. Not sure how accurate this is, so going to let the juice settle and take
a reading after a couple hours.

Few hours later now (the thick pulpy stuff has settled some)… `OG: 1.100`. Pitched the yeast and
stirred for another 5m. Covered the fermenter hole with cheesecloth and set by the AC. It stays
around 79 there.
