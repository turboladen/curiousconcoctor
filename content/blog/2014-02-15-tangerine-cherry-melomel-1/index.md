## The Story

I went back to the [Vineyard Farmer’s Market](http://www.vineyardfarmersmarket.com/) again and
couldn’t help but think that a mead from some of the
[Murcott Tangerines](http://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/ch078) I passed could be quite refreshing for the
coming hot months. It didn’t take long for me to walk away from there with a bag full.

Since I knew I’d be needing another one-gallon carboy, I headed back to Whole Foods for yet another
gallon carboy that happened to be filled with apple juice. While there, I thought I’d see if they
had tart cherries that I could add to give the whole thing a little kick. Sure enough, they did.

## Recipe

Since I’m winging it again, this could end up being a crappy recipe, but this is turning out to be
the fun part of the whole process–deciding what flavors to put together. Here’s what I came up with.

- 14 Murcott Tangerines
- 1 7oz thing of Dried Tart Cherries from WF (minus about 8 :))
- 3 lbs Wildflower Honey from [Kingsburg Honey](http://www.kingsburghoney.com/)
- 1 packet [Red Star Première Cuvée](http://www.midwestsupplies.com/red-star-premier-cuvee.html)

I’m bummed I didn’t take a picture of it; the wildflower honey is a medium color with a nice, rich,
round flavor. Really looking forward to the flavors this imparts.

I should also add that this is the first time I’ll be using
[the "no heat" method](http://www.washingtonwinemaker.com/blog/2007/06/05/making-mead-the-controversy-over-boiling/)
for the honey. It’s less work, but more importantly to me, it is less destructive to the honey, thus
more natural. If honey can, on its own, stave off bacteria, then I shouldn’t need to sterilize it.
If [Ken Schramm](http://www.amazon.com/Ken-Schramm/e/B001KCJ71K) advocates it, then I’m all for it.

## Log

### 2/15, 1pm.

Washed tangerines in soap and water, lightly scrubbed with the veggie scrubber to get dirt off.
Peeled (no pith!) 8 tangerines (just under 1 cup, loosely packed) and put in cheesecloth sack thing.
Juiced by hand about 8 of them, then juiced the remaining using the juicer (much easier!).

We recently got a
[Breville BJS600XL Juicer](http://www.amazon.com/Breville-BJS600XL-Fountain-Masticating-Juicer/dp/B008P8LAVW/ref=sr_1_1?s=home-garden&ie=UTF8&qid=1406078590&sr=1-1&keywords=breville+bjs600xl),
a masticating juicer, which crushes instead shreds (like centrifugal juicers), thus extracting more
juice and leaving the fruit a little more in tact. I like to think of it like a twist on a fruit
press. This is the first time I’ve used it, really, but think it’ll be getting a lot more use in the
future.
