## The Story

It’s fig week here in Fresno, which means the annual Fig Fest, local restaurants featuring fig
dishes, and, well, figs. There’s quite a few varieties of figs, which I’ve come to love over the
past couple of years. As a kid, they seemed kinda gross and weird (coming from a kid who got
sardines and frozen peas in his lunch), so I generally passed on eating them until a couple years
ago. I was at a Farmer’s Market and someone coaxed me into buying some green figs–I was totally
taken back by the way it sort of popped open in my mouth and slowly gushed out, oozing yummy sweet
flesh–then seeing the pink insides… it was like a whole new experience.

The problem with figs, though, is that when you buy a ton of them, you have to eat a ton of them.
They don’t last. If you’re lucky, you get maybe 3 days out of them in your fridge, then they’re on
their way out. They don’t pop and gush anymore, they mostly just mush about. The flavor will stay a
little while longer, but it’s the flesh that really sets figs apart, in my opinion. There’s really
nothing else like figs.

So what a perfect time to make a fig mead, no? I love figs and honey in yogurt. So freaking good.
Fig mead is 2/3 of that and just a slightly different format, so it must be delicious, right? Well,
I’m going to find out. After eating a couple few figs, then juicing a few more, I decided that I’d
like to end up with a concoction that’s slightly spicy–or at least elements of spice–instead of
something light and fizzy for summer. I think figs could go either way. It’ll probably be still and
I’ll aim for full body.

## Ingredients

I found two recipes for fig melomels online–it’s good to have a reference–and a few more recipes for
fig wines. I usually try to find recipes for things that I want to make so I can a) see how much
fruit they use, and b) see what yeast they used. Here’s what I’m starting with:

- 22 lbs. Black Mission Figs
- 1 G. Orange Blossom honey
- 2 vanilla beans
- 1 stick ceylon cinnamon
- Raisins
- 1 1/4 T. pectic enzyme
- ~2 G. water
- Lalvin RC-212

## Log

### 8/9.

Soaked 18 lbs. figs in KMeta in the sink for about 20 minutes, then rinsed, cut off stems, and
juiced. About half-way through the figs, I added the honey and stirred it in, then juice the
remaining figs. I considered heating the figs to maybe help with flavor extraction, but I think this
time I’m going raw. I want to go as simple as possible before making big changes. Lastly, I added
the pectic enzyme to about 2 cups of water, let that sit for about 15 minutes, then added that to
the must and stirred for a while. Put the lid on the Brew Bucket, then covered the airlock hole with
cheesecloth and moved the bucket next to the A/C vent.

Lots of (the few) recipes I came across for reference used Montrachet yeast, but after poking around
the interwebs more, I’m deciding to steer clear of that right now; I can’t find the link now, but it
sounds like due to the high temps of our summers here, the Montrachet could produce some extra,
unwanted phenols (which produce a band-aid scent and throat-burn in the short term). Seems like that
wouldn’t be the case if I could ferment under 70 degrees.

So now, I’m flip-flopping from using a 71B-1122 and ICV D47. Both will ferment away some of the
malic acid, which I think I’d like to avoid doing, but the only other two options I have (read:
“that are in my refrigerator”) are Bourgovin RC-212 and Red Star Premier Cuvée and neither of those
sound very suited to the figs. It sounds like the 71B will do a “better” job on the malic acid, so
since I’d like to keep some of that green-apple tartness (from the malic acid), I think I’m leaning
towards the ICV D47. Additionally, I like the idea of the alleged tropical, spicy, and citrusy
flavors that the D47 can bring. I also like the idea that the D47’s tolerance is around 12%, and
since this sucker must be pretty high in sugar, this should leave the final mead on the sweet side
(I think drying this one out would really get rid of all of that delicate fig flavor) without any
intervention.

It’s quite sweet and already a bit complex. I can’t wait to see how this pans out.

After thinking and reading for the past day, it sounds like the D47 may also ferment away some of
the fruit flavor and enhance the honey flavor—this is the exact opposite for what I want. I’m
worried that the 71B would leave this too buttery—I want it to have a slight bit of spice, and the
more I read, the more I’m intrigued how the RC-212 would perform. I was pleasantly surprised to see
that the fig juice had, over night, gone from light brownish to a nice, light red, which also
influences my yeast choice here: the RC-212 is used for Pinot Noirs, which seems like a relatively
desirable direction to head in with this concoction.

Lastly, I’ve decided to add the spices–vanilla and cinnamon—to help support the fig flavor. I’m
anticipating the fig flavor to be light, so anything I can do to help that out is warranted.

So I stirred a few times today, then pitched the yeast tonight and stirred some more. Added the
raisins and 1 vanilla bean (split and scraped; added the whole bean to the must too). Put the lid
back on, with the cheesecloth-covered hole, and moved back to the A/C vent again.
