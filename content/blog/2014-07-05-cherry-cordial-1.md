## The Story

My co-concocting friend went out of town for the weekend and on his way back informed me that he’d
picked up something like 20 lbs. of Bing cherries (!). Shortly after, he dropped off a hefty grocery
bag full of them for me to experiment with–which I was more than happy to do.

Since receiving my Brew Bucket, I’d sort of decided in the back of my brain that from now on I’d
focus on doing 5 gallon batches of whatever it is I was going to concoct. Also, since most of what
I’ve read about doing cherry meads or ciders suggests using some variety of tart/baking cherries
(Bings are sweet cherries), this didn’t seem like the time to give it a shot.

I asked my wife what sounded good: she said a “cherry cordial because [something about Anne of Green
Gables?]”. That reminded me about the “Cherry Euphoria” recipe that Ken Schramm gives in his book,
[_The Compleat Meadmaker_](http://www.amazon.com/The-Compleat-Meadmaker-Production-Award-winning/dp/0937381802);
there’s not a whole lot to it, but here’s the ingredients:

- 750ml cheap vodka
- 4 c. fresh or frozen Red Tart pitted cherries
- 2 c. table sugar

It sounded simple and tasty, and was a new path that I could curiously meander down. Decision made.

## Recipe

- 4 lbs. [Bing cherries](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_cherry)
- 0.5 L.
  [Knudsen Organic Just Tart Cherry juice](http://www.rwknudsenfamily.com/products/just-juice/organic-just-tart-cherry)
- 4 vanilla beans
- 1 1/3 c. white sugar
- 1 c. brown sugar
- 1/2 c. maple syrup
- 1 packet [Lalvin 71B-1122](http://www.lalvinyeast.com/71B.asp)

I pitted the cherries as soon as I got them and put them all in 1 gallon Ziploc bag, then stuck them
in the freezer for a week or so. The 1/2 liter of tart juice got added to the recipe simply because
I noticed it in my fridge at the time I started making this. Really, I probably could’ve stood to
use a bit more of this, but since this was a sort of shotgun concocting, I went with what I had on
hand.

As for the sugars, I realize I could’ve just gone with straight white sugar, but I thought that
since I was adding the vanilla, having a bit more rounded sweetness could be nice–hence the brown
sugar and maple syrup. And really, the syrup wasn’t planned either–since it seemed like I’d
fermented away all of my flavor, this seemed like a good way to add something interesting back in.

Lastly, the yeast. If I followed Ken Schramm’s recipe, there would’ve been no yeast at all involved
in this whole thing, but that just seemed kinda boring to me. I’d been curious to find out what
fermenting cherries is like, and doing the fermenting seemed like a way to get a bit more of an
interesting flavor for this concoction. In retrospect, I probably should’ve skipped on this, but so
far the result has been different and interesting.
