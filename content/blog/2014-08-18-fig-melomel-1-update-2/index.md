Sometimes racking really sucks. Not usually, but when it’s a mess it can get to be no fun. …and this
fig mead is turning out to be the messiest I’ve done so far.

I wish I had taken more pictures of this whole ordeal, but it was late and hot and I was getting
cranky so that all kinda went out the window. The Brew Bucket was chock full of figs (in a 5G
painter’s bag), which I figured would make the racking process super simple: just attach the hose to
the spigot, put the other end into the fresh carboy, open the spigot valve and wait. Nope. Well, I
did that, but only ended up with a little over 1G of stuff in the fresh carboy–not exactly what I
was expecting from 20 lbs of figs, 12 lbs of honey, and 1G of water.

So I figured that the fruit in the painters bag had just absorbed a lot of the must and thus if I
should just be able to squish the bag enough to get a good portion of that mead into my carboy. I
hadn’t considered, however, how much work it would be to hold that bag full of 20 lbs of figs, 12
lbs of honey, and 1G of water–minus only the 1G of mead that I’d already transferred. It took
probably 20 minutes for me to realize that this approach wasn’t sustainable, so I changed
strategies. I took my ginormous funnel, stuck it in the top of the carboy, pulled the bag of fruit
out of the Brew Bucket and shoved it into the funnel.

It sucked. Royally. Yeast is slippery and gross and likes to clog stuff up, so when you squeeze your
painters bag full of fruit, etc., it squirts liquid out of places that you don’t expect. I had
figgy, yeasty must all over the kitchen. The walls, the floor, the counters. That too was slow and
arduous, but after about 45 more minutes, I got most of the liquid transferred (along with a ton of
solids, unfortunately) and just dumped the rest down the drain. Here’s what my hands looked like by
this point:

Additionally, I’d learned this past week (and meant to post about it) that unripe figs contain a
fair amount of natural latex. Yes–latex. I’d wondered why all of the equipment I’d used to start
this mead ended up all sticky and gooey. Do some googling and you’ll find all sorts of interesting
stuff about figs + latex. I can’t find the source, but I recall reading that only unripe figs will
ooze white, milky liquid when you cut them–that’s the latex; once ripe, cuts will no longer yield
this liquid. Farm workers that deal with figs have to wear protection (<– lulz), as the latex is
toxic and can, at the very least, cause some serious skin irritation. If you’re allergic to the
stuff, it can get pretty gnarly, causing conjunctivitis (pink eye) and some other weird things that
I don’t remember. In any case, I had to scrub the Brew Bucket with Barkeeper’s Friend in order to
get the gooey stuff out. Check it:
