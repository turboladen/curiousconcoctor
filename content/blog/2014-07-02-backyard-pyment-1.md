## The Story

Simply due to the fact that I have this blog and you’re reading it right now, it should come as no
surprise to you to read that I’ve been mulling over for quite some time how I should purpose the
grapes I have growing in my backyard. My wife has little interest in eating them, so they were
pretty much left to me for the picking–when the time came for that. And it came a bit unexpectedly.

Since we’d moved in to this house, three years ago now, I’d expected grapes to come off of this vine
that seems haphazardly nestled between the garage, the fence, and a built-in brick barbeque. I’d
actually not given the vine much care, mostly because of the weird space that it’s growing in–a
seemingly great spot for bugs and such to get all up on me. This past year, however–mostly prompted
by my new interest in concocting goodies from nature–I decided to show the vine some love, and,
leading up to day, there’d been a fair amount of bunches of berries that have shown up and looked
promising.

It’d been some weeks since I actually checked on these guys, but I was out in the garden, poking
around, and gave them some attention. Amanda (whose day job is spent doing R&D on grapes) wasn’t
sure, but had guessed that these guys were Red Flames–table grapes that are usually ripe no earlier
than the beginning of July. When I checked on them today, they looked shriveled, varied greatly in
size and color, and even had some mold on them. This was far from how they looked just a couple
weeks ago. I figured that leaving them on the vine wasn’t going to help at all, so it was time to
pull them off and get to concocting.

I’d really planned on doing a wine, regardless of the varietal, but after cleaning the berries up
and juicing them, I only had a single liter of juice, which wouldn’t exactly get you a party, and
really would maybe yield a single 750ml bottle after everything was said and done. Thus, I decided
on doing the pyment. Adding honey and water would get me the same level brix, but just a little
diluted flavor, and a full gallon’s worth.

Here we go.

## Recipe

- 2.2L grape juice
- 1.75lb. Kingsburg Wildflower honey
- 1/2t pectic enzyme
- 1/8t Kmeta
- 1 packet Lalvin K1-V116 yeast
- 9g medium toast, American Oak chips

## Log

Washed berries in dish soap, rinsed twice. Soaked in Kmeta for about 5-10m, the rinsed. Juiced
berries; left a bunch of stems on. Gravity was just under 1.100, which
[http://winemakermag.com/your-first-wine-from-fresh-grapes](http://winemakermag.com/your-first-wine-from-fresh-grapes)
said indicates good brix.
