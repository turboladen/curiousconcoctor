## The Story

I wasn’t necessarily looking to do another Pink Lady cider so soon, but I a) just got my beautiful,
new [Brew Bucket from Ss Brewing Technologies](http://www.ssbrewtech.com/products/brewbucket), down
in Temecula, CA, and b) ran across trays of Pink Ladies for sale at Costco for much cheaper than I
paid for them the last time around. I couldn’t resist and bought 7 trays of them, then promptly went
down to my LHBS, [Bencomo’s Homebrew Supply](http://www.bencomoshomebrewsupply.com/), and picked up
some more
[White Labs WLP775 English Cider yeast](http://www.whitelabs.com/yeast/wlp775-english-cider-yeast).

<image src="IMG_2537.jpeg" width="100%" alt="brew bucket">

I realized shortly after starting this concoction that making cider like this is so super simple
and, largely in part due to this simplicity, really rewarding to make. I love the feeling of taking
simple, raw ingredients from nature (or from Costco), throwing them together, waiting for a bit,
then enjoying how nature takes those ingredients and transforms them into a new form of yumminess.

## Recipe

- 35lbs Pink Lady apples
- White Labs WLP775 English Cider yeast

## Log

Washed then sanitized the Brew Bucket for 20 mins. Soaked apples in fruit and veggie wash for 5
mins. Sanitized the juicer with KMeta. The most painstaking part of this process was prepping the
apples so they’d fit in
[my juicer](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B008P8LAVW/ref=s9_simh_gw_p79_d3_i1?pf_rd_m=ATVPDKIKX0DER&pf_rd_s=center-3&pf_rd_r=0S5NCRCF3WD9HSVYPACB&pf_rd_t=101&pf_rd_p=1688200422&pf_rd_i=507846).
It’s totally worth it though. In fact, working with the fruit like this is part of what makes the
payoff so great.

<image src="IMG_2539.jpeg" width="100%" alt="whoops">
(whoops)

After slicing, I juiced most of the 35lbs of apples; ended up with just under 3G juice. My wife
started making apple butter with the pulp (added 1c brown sugar and heated)–while doing so, I
realized I wanted pulp for the wort (tannins!), so I stole enough to fill my muslin bag pretty full.
Added the bag to the cider (no marbles). Cider temp is at 97F now (18:35).

<image src="IMG_2540.jpeg" width="100%" alt="foam">

Temp only down to 86F at 12am. Pitched the whole vial of yeast, which had been sitting out for about
8 hours now (that was unintentional). Stirred a ton. Took a reading, then realized there were tons
of pulp in it (that read 1.050), then redid but strained as I poured into the test cylinder.
`OG: 1.048`. Moved to the living room where it was a bit cooler.
