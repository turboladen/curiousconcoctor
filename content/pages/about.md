Hi, I’m Steve! This is where I write down whatever I’m concocting, mostly so I remember what I did
(and what to never do again).

## How this all started

Lemons. So many lemons. I had Meyer lemons coming out of my ears, and I’d already done the
limoncello thing (which I like, but there’s only so much one guy needs). So I did a little
interwebbing, found [Sima](@/blog/2013-12-the-lemons-started-it-all-sima/index.md), and figured, why
not? Next thing I knew I was fascinated by fermentation. And by how the stuff actually tastes.

Then it hit me: I live in California’s Central Valley, so there’s a ridiculous amount of fruit
around to play with. I started with mead because it was rare back then (you couldn’t just find it in
a store, at least not where I lived). The craft beer world was exploding at the same time, and it
was kinda nice to be into something that wasn’t that. Beer was easy to find, so why make it? Also,
all those big pots and cooking and cooling looked like a lot of work.

## How I go about it

Basically everything is an experiment, and I like using local, in-season fruit. At first I wanted
the simplest process and the most natural ingredients I could get away with, so no packaged yeast
nutrients (that felt contrived). Raisins to the rescue! They bring the nitrogen and nutrients the
yeast wants. Eventually I came around to the usual modern stuff, nutrients and clarifying agents and
all, because it turns out they give slightly more consistent, tastier results. Shocking, I know.

My favorite part by far is coming up with the recipe: picking flavor combos, then the yeast and
profile to go with them. That’s where the name comes from. I’d rather keep trying something new than
get really good at one thing.

## Where things are now

Confession: I’ve slowed way down, because it turns out I like making stuff more than drinking it.
There are still tens of bottles from the late 2010s in my basement that I’m not crazy keen to open.
When I want a drink it’s usually a beer or a whisky, not something fruity-sweet. I do like wine
though, which is part of why I planted wine grapes in the backyard. Growing them is hands down the
hardest part of making wine, and I have a ton more respect for growers now.

I’m not done, though! I still have most of my gear, and I’ll probably go through another brewing
phase someday. We’ll see.

Happy Concocting!
